# Structure Rules

These rules apply to long-form explanatory, analytical, and argumentative writing. They define structural quality without prescribing a particular publication format, language, heading style, or authorial voice.

## Core rules

1. One main question should drive the article. Major sections must serve that question rather than accumulate unrelated observations.

2. The article needs progression. Each major section should add a mechanism, distinction, boundary, implication, counterargument, or other new analytical work.

3. Each major section must have a clear argumentative function. Avoid sections that mix several unrelated jobs without a reason.

4. Do not reveal conclusions earlier than the reasoning can support them. Structure should let the reader see how the answer is reached.

5. A local analogy, example, scene, historical episode, or comparison must do analytical work. It may clarify a mechanism or distinction, but it cannot substitute for evidence.

6. A whole-article central frame or narrative anchor is optional. Use one only when it can organize multiple parts of the reasoning, not merely decorate the opening.

7. A later structural upgrade—from a local case to a broader institutional, market, technical, or social implication—must be earned by the earlier mechanism.

8. Responses and recommendations must address the problem actually established by the article.

9. The ending should answer, revise, qualify, or meaningfully return to the opening problem. It should not merely repeat the thesis.

10. An article may mix local techniques, but it should have one primary architecture.

## Optional architecture examples

These are examples, not required templates.

### Question-driven

```text
problem
→ competing explanations
→ mechanism
→ boundary
→ implication
→ answer
```

### Case-driven

```text
concrete case
→ anomaly or tension
→ mechanism
→ broader pattern
→ boundary
→ implication
```

### Historical-contrast

```text
earlier case
→ later case
→ important difference
→ mechanism behind the difference
→ present implication
```

### Mechanism-driven

```text
claim
→ mechanism A
→ mechanism B
→ interaction
→ boundary
→ conclusion
```

G3 may choose a hybrid when one mode is clearly primary and another serves as a secondary device.

## Compression standard

A good structure can be compressed into a short reasoning chain without collapsing into a list of topics. The structure serves the question; the question does not exist to fill a template.
