# Mechanical Writing Pattern Rules

---
status: active
scope: shared-writing-rule
---

These semantic rules support G7. They are intentionally language-neutral. A pattern is only a candidate for cleanup when context shows that it adds no necessary meaning.

## RULE-001: Empty emphasis

Candidate trigger:
Repeated emphasis words or phrases that increase rhetorical force without adding information.

Context judgment:
Remove or rewrite only when factual meaning, uncertainty, time, degree, and voice remain unchanged.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep emphasis that carries real contrast, degree, stance, or genre function.

Preservation constraints:
Do not alter claim strength.

## RULE-002: Mechanical trend framing

Candidate trigger:
Repeated trend shells used where the underlying change is already stated directly.

Context judgment:
Simplify only when trend direction and strength remain intact.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep when the trend itself is analytically important.

Preservation constraints:
Do not invent magnitude, duration, or causality.

## RULE-003: Formulaic reversal

Candidate trigger:
Repeated contrast formulas that manufacture drama rather than express a real logical exclusion or correction.

Context judgment:
Rewrite as direct comparison when the logical relation remains unchanged.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep genuine correction, rebuttal, or exclusion.

Preservation constraints:
Do not erase competing explanations.

## RULE-004: Redundant meta-signposting

Candidate trigger:
Phrases that announce a conclusion, restatement, or importance but the following sentence merely repeats what is already clear.

Context judgment:
Remove the signpost only when no logical relation is lost.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep signposting that marks a real inference, condition, scope change, or hierarchy.

Preservation constraints:
Do not remove necessary logical structure.

## RULE-005: Fixed paragraph or section closure

Candidate trigger:
Several paragraphs or sections end with the same punch-line or thesis-restatement pattern.

Context judgment:
Clean only closures that repeat existing content.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep a closing sentence that adds a new implication, boundary, or transition.

Preservation constraints:
Do not remove new analytical work.

## RULE-006: Mechanical parallelism and list symmetry

Candidate trigger:
Repeated three-part lists, syntactic symmetry, or parallel sentences used mainly to make prose look polished.

Context judgment:
Break the pattern only when no required item or relationship is lost.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep real steps, legal elements, factual lists, or deliberate rhetoric.

Preservation constraints:
Do not manufacture irregularity for its own sake.

## RULE-007: Abstract padding

Candidate trigger:
Dense abstract nouns obscure actions, actors, mechanisms, or relationships already present in the text.

Context judgment:
Use the existing concrete action or relationship when it preserves meaning.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep abstractions that are themselves the object of analysis or established terminology.

Preservation constraints:
Do not add new examples or facts.

## RULE-008: Explanatory throat-clearing

Candidate trigger:
Introductory phrases that merely delay a statement without limiting or framing it.

Context judgment:
Delete only when the proposition and its scope remain unchanged.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep phrases that genuinely limit perspective, uncertainty, or domain.

Preservation constraints:
Do not broaden the claim.

## RULE-009: Connector inertia

Candidate trigger:
Repeated connectors or emphasis markers that do not carry real causal, temporal, or logical work.

Context judgment:
Remove only redundant instances.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep necessary sequence, causality, duration, or contrast.

Preservation constraints:
Do not change relation or order.

## RULE-010: Manufactured irregularity

Candidate trigger:
An edit is proposed only to make prose appear more human by randomizing sentence, paragraph, or section length.

Authorized action:
Do not make that edit.

Preservation constraints:
Irregularity is not evidence of human authorship.

## RULE-011: Empty promotional evaluation

Candidate trigger:
Generic praise or dismissal appears without a concrete mechanism, result, or criterion.

Context judgment:
Delete the evaluation or use concrete information already present.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep established terminology, quotations, or degree judgments that carry real meaning.

Preservation constraints:
Do not invent quantitative support.

## RULE-012: Boilerplate opening or closing

Candidate trigger:
A generic opening or closing phrase contributes no real time, condition, inference, or conclusion.

Context judgment:
Remove the boilerplate while preserving the actual content.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep real temporal framing, cross-section synthesis, or a new conclusion.

Preservation constraints:
Do not remove necessary context.

## RULE-013: Fake progression

Candidate trigger:
A progression formula presents two statements as a deeper second step even though they are merely parallel or repetitive.

Context judgment:
Rewrite as ordinary coordination when hierarchy is not real.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep genuine expansion in scope, strength, depth, or consequence.

Preservation constraints:
Do not flatten a real progression.

## RULE-014: Rhythm-only question and answer

Candidate trigger:
A rhetorical question immediately answered by an obvious statement only to create cadence.

Context judgment:
Remove the question when it is not a real question driving the argument.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep the article's actual main question, a genuine section question, or purposeful rhetoric.

Preservation constraints:
Do not remove real problem framing.

## RULE-015: Unnecessary nominalization

Candidate trigger:
A simple action is wrapped in an abstract noun construction without changing meaning.

Context judgment:
Prefer the direct action when actor, object, condition, and strength remain unchanged.

Keep / exceptions:
Keep established technical, legal, or disciplinary terminology.

Preservation constraints:
Sentence-level cleanup only; do not use this rule to restructure paragraphs or arguments.
