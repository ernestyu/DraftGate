# Writing Base Rules

---
status: active
scope: shared-writing-rule
---

These rules remain active across all writing gates and are intentionally minimal and language-neutral.

1. The article must ultimately be driven by one main question.
2. Do not invent facts, sources, data, examples, or quotations.
3. Keep factual claims, analytical judgments, and uncertain inferences distinguishable.
4. Do not turn a contested or conditional conclusion into a certainty for rhetorical effect.
5. Preserve user-frozen titles, facts, formats, and other explicit constraints unless the current gate authorizes changing them.
6. A gate may modify only its Allowed scope. Forbidden scope takes priority over opportunistic improvement.
7. Reduce reader effort without deleting necessary mechanisms, conditions, or uncertainty.
8. A strong long-form article should leave general readers with the problem and conclusion, intermediate readers with the mechanism, and expert readers able to see the boundaries.
9. After completing one gate, stop. Do not execute the next gate in the same invocation.
10. Do not impose a default authorial persona. First person, third person, formal, conversational, and other voice choices are document-level decisions unless the user explicitly freezes them.
